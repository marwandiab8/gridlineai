    What was claimed, and the consultant's two questions

    Area and depthOne pit on the CB-4 lead at the tie-in: about 3.6 m long × 2.4 m wide × 2.4 m deep below the June 22 subgrade ◇, about 21 m³, and about 3.0 m below the finished asphalt. Sketch SK-RWL-01 marks it up in plan, in a section across the pit and in a profile along the lead.

    Labour hoursThe hours are from Road-Ex's signed tickets 2570 and 2571: each day the foreman 10 h, 3 labourers 30 h, and the excavator and rubber-tire with their operators 10 h each. Both tickets cover only this tie-in, the "refueling station drainage" named in CD-007 §3.2. None of the hours are for the irrigation point of connection. Rates and dollars are from Road-Ex's CD 007 cost summary and invoice 62223.

    Two daysThe lead was already buried and deep, so it had to be dug back up in a trench box, cut open and spliced by hand, then backfilled in compacted lifts under the future drive aisle.

    To confirm from the field (the ◇ values)
    • Depth. About 2.4 m below the June 22 subgrade is worked out from the GEN-1 inverts (storage tank INV 248.990, 675 main at 0.59 % and 0.80 %), the 640 mm pavement and CB-4 T/G 252.87. Confirm the depth Road-Ex dug, from the foreman or the June 22 photos.
    • Pit size. 3.6 × 2.4 m assumes a 10 ft box plus working room. Confirm the length and width dug.
    • Trench box. The invoice says 8×10 aluminium, read here as 8 ft high × 10 ft long. Its rental invoice isn't in Road-Ex's backup; ask for it.
    • Tie-in point and stub. About 6.5 m from CB-4 is scaled off GEN-1, which was issued Jun 25, after the work. Confirm where the wye went and how high the stub came up.
    • Method. The order of work here is built from the two tickets and normal practice. Check it against what you saw on site before it goes out.
